The browser twin tunes an order-up-to policy online as a lightweight stand-in for the offline PPO agent, so it is directional, not identical to the reported PPO numbers below.

Key findings

Rigorous, reproducible evaluation: PPO agents trained with and without a demand forecast across multiple items, two forecast models (Linear vs RandomForest), repeated seeds, and robustness scenarios (noisy forecast, carbon-aware). Numbers below are the reported PPO results.

💡 Insight

Forecast information pays off

Average service-level reward rises +0.146 (~19%) with the forecast, significant at t = 4.54 — a real signal, unlike the near-zero effect in the original un-normalised design.

💡 Insight

Benefit is heterogeneous

Per item the advantage is −0.04 / +0.29 / +0.19: on some demand profiles the demand-blind baseline is already near-optimal, so the forecast adds little or slightly hurts.

💡 Insight

Availability > model choice

Linear and RandomForest are statistically indistinguishable (MAE 0.99 vs 1.00). Having a forecast in the state matters far more than which forecaster produced it.

What the model does

1 · Forecast

Predict demand

Lag-7 features → next-day demand with LinearRegression / RandomForest.

2 · Digital twin

Inventory env

Order-up-to control with lead time, so anticipating demand is required — this is what makes the forecast useful.

3 · Learn

PPO policy

Normalised observations + a bounded service-level reward give stable training.

4 · Evaluate

FC vs baseline

Compare across items, seeds, and robustness scenarios with significance testing.

Full derivations, illustrations, and the complete results-and-discussion are in the paper. The entire pipeline is reproducible from NEW_PIB_2026.ipynb / pib_rl_local.py.
